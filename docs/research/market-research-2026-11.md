# Planners ignore the engineer's real workday

As of 15 November 2026, no planner on the market is built for software developers or quality engineers. The visible part of your vision (drag tasks into and out of a calendar, click a day to quick-add) is already standard in Sunsama, Akiflow, Morgen and Todoist, so it will not set you apart. The differentiation sits in the "powerful underneath" layer. **No personal planner checked has a native Azure DevOps integration.** **None nests deeper than TickTick's 5 levels or Todoist's 4.** **None lets you drag a reusable task bundle onto a day.** And "two-way" Jira sync means status write-back at most. The opening is real but narrow, and it comes with four risks. Incumbents could add Azure DevOps (ADO) at any time. Super Productivity already offers free, open-source Jira, GitHub and GitLab sync. Enterprise admins can block third-party apps. And no community evidence was found that QA engineers want test-pass templates inside a personal planner. The practical path is to build GitHub first (easiest API, and it gets the sync engine working), ADO second as the headline differentiator (with a personal access token fallback), and Jira third for parity. Position the app as the calm personal layer over the team's tracker, not as another AI calendar, and price it at about $8–12/month with a free tier. Of the six names, **Hexday is the only low-risk candidate**. Waggle and Combly collide with live productivity products. None of this is legal advice. Evidence caveat: many accessible reviews are written by competitors, and Reddit, G2 and Capterra could not be fetched directly, so sentiment findings are indicative only.

## Calendar dragging is table stakes; engineering depth is missing everywhere

The category splits into three groups. The first is premium daily planners: Sunsama, Akiflow, Morgen, and the cheaper Routine. These treat drag-to-timebox as core. Sunsama lets you drag Jira issues straight onto the calendar or press `S` to add one to today ([Sunsama Help](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/integrations/jira)). Morgen's AI Planner proposes 1–7 days of time blocks and schedules nothing until you approve ([Morgen](https://www.morgen.so/ai-planner)). The second group is AI auto-schedulers, Motion and Reclaim, which place and reflow tasks for you. The third is established task managers, Todoist and TickTick, which reached near-parity on manual calendar dragging in late 2026: Todoist shipped durations without dates, press-and-drag task creation and a filtered "Plan" sidebar between September and November ([Todoist changelog](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/2026-changelog-HD3jJAtLd)). Todoist is the closest benchmark for your quick-add and drag interactions. One gesture is unconfirmed everywhere: no product's documentation shows dragging a block off the calendar back into the backlog as a first-class action.

The market has also thinned in ways that matter to you. **Clockwise shut down on 27 March 2026 after a Salesforce acqui-hire and deleted user data without export** ([Carly, competitor blog](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/is-clockwise-shutting-down/)). Height, a developer-oriented project management (PM) tool, closed on 24 September 2025 ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/3/height-project-management-tool-to-shut-down-by-september-2025/)). Motion has moved upmarket toward an "agentic work suite" for businesses ([Temporal, competitor blog](https://temporal.day/blog/motion-ai-agents-individual-users-2026)). Amie has pivoted to AI meeting notes ([Amie changelog](https://amie.so/changelog)). The two products closest to your niche are small. nocal is the only planner found that names developers as an audience, but it has no Jira or GitHub integration ([nocal](https://nocal.app)). Super Productivity is an open-source tool that syncs Jira, GitHub and GitLab, tracks time and adds calendar events, but has no ADO integration ([Super Productivity](https://super-productivity.com/integrations/)). Things 3 remains the design benchmark for "simple surface" polish. It is a one-time purchase, Apple-only and single-user ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/things-3-review/)).

## Users leave over price, babysitting AI and read-only imports

**Price is the most frequent complaint**, and it hits hardest in the $15–49/month premium tier. "Expensive pricing" is the most common con on Sunsama's Product Hunt reviews (5 of 21) ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/sunsama/reviews)). A long-time Motion user reported a 30% renewal increase in January 2026 ([Carly, competitor](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/motion-ai-review/)). Todoist's December 2025 rise of its monthly Pro price from $5 to $7 dented goodwill ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/best-todoist-alternatives-3615857/)). Reviewers also see a pattern behind the price complaints: people who quit Sunsama had usually stopped the daily planning ritual first, and then the price no longer felt justified ([The Business Dive](https://thebusinessdive.com/sunsama-review)). So churn starts with a lapsed habit, and price becomes the stated reason. An app that is cheap and guilt-free to come back to retains better.

**Weak mobile apps** come second. Motion's App Store listing itself says the app is best used on a computer; it is rated 3.8 on Google Play ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1580440623)). Akiflow users report a laggy mobile app that lost drag-to-calendar ([Saner.AI, competitor](https://blog.saner.ai/akiflow-reviews)). **Auto-scheduling splits opinion along the line of control.** Fans call Reclaim a game changer ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/reclaim-ai)). Critics describe Motion placing tasks over busy events and moving them repeatedly. One App Store reviewer wrote, "if I have to supervise the scheduler, it defeats the purpose" ([Carly, competitor](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/motion-ai-review/)). One comparison blog reports 11 reschedules in a single day causing real anxiety ([getdailytoolbox](https://getdailytoolbox.com/productivity/motion-vs-reclaim-ai/)). Developers are a sceptical audience: **66% of Stack Overflow's 2025 respondents named "almost right" AI output as their top AI frustration** ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/stack-overflow-data-reveals-the-hidden-productivity-tax-of-almost-right-ai-code)).

For developers, the pain is less any single planner than how their work is scattered across tools. **Atlassian's State of DevEx 2025 (n=3,500) found 50% of developers lose 10+ hours a week to non-coding friction**, with context switching between tools among the top causes ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/developer/developer-experience-report-2025)). Interruptions matter too. GitHub and Microsoft's older Good Day study (about 40 engineers) found an 82% chance of a good day with minimal interruptions, against 7% when interrupted most of the day ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2021/05/25/developers_interruptions_github/)). On Hacker News, developers say Jira doesn't reflect their real day, and that keeping a separate personal system adds overhead ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37140883)). Incumbent integrations do little to help. Sunsama copies a Jira issue's due date and estimate once, at import, and never updates them; subtasks added in Sunsama stay invisible to Jira ([Sunsama Help](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/integrations/jira)). Its public roadmap carries unshipped requests to "fully sync Jira" and to pull Jira checklists in as subtasks ([Sunsama roadmap](https://roadmap.sunsama.com/improvements/p/fully-sync-jira-so-i-can-use-this-product)). In 2024 it told users that integration requests are for gauging interest, not commitments ([Sunsama roadmap](https://roadmap.sunsama.com/integrations/p/why-has-there-been-no-progress-on-integrations)). Demand for ADO shows up in adjacent tools: a GitKraken request for ADO Boards has 133 votes, and ClickUp has a request for ADO pull request tracking ([GitKraken](https://feedback.gitkraken.com/suggestions/192821/azure-devops-user-story-bugs-integration); [ClickUp](https://feedback.clickup.com/feature-requests/p/azure-devops-pull-request-tracking)).

The weakest evidence concerns your own audience. **No QA-engineer community evidence was found at all**; r/QualityAssurance could not be reached. Demand for reusable regression and release checklists comes from vendor content: TestRail, Manifestly, checklist.gg. These sources do describe a real pattern: spreadsheet QA tracking breaks down after a handful of releases, and a manually typed "pass" cell goes stale ([Manifestly](https://app.manifest.ly/public/checklists/qa-testing-checklist); [Autonoma](https://getautonoma.com/blog/release-readiness-checklist)). Guidance on "gentle", guilt-free design also comes mostly from vendors of ADHD apps. They recommend "carried over" wording instead of red overdue labels ([TaskFade](https://www.taskfade.com/blog/adhd-shame-cycle-task-apps)). That suits your design tone, but it is not independent evidence.

## Five gaps remain open, and Azure DevOps is the widest

The verified gaps reinforce each other. First, **Azure DevOps**: Sunsama, Akiflow and Motion reach it only through Zapier ([Zapier](https://zapier.com/apps/akiflow/integrations/azure-devops)), and Super Productivity does not list it. Microsoft-shop users are left to build Power Automate bridges, which need a premium connector and miss some updates ([Crosstown Tech](https://crosstowntech.com/apps/planner-azure-devops-power-automate/)). Second, **deep nesting**: Todoist stops at 4 levels ([Todoist Help](https://todoist.com/help/articles/introduction-to-sub-tasks-kMamDo)). Sunsama flattens ClickUp hierarchies beyond 2 levels on import ([Sunsama roadmap](https://roadmap.sunsama.com/improvements/p/clickup-and-todoist-nested-subtasks-levels)). TickTick loses the hierarchy once a task is on the calendar. So "epic → story → task → sub-task → checklist" has no home, and how nested items appear when scheduled is an open design problem worth solving well. Third, **drag-and-drop task bundles**: Todoist's templates create whole projects from a gallery or CSV, and nothing lets you drop a release checklist onto Thursday. Fourth, **pull request (PR) review queues and hierarchical sync**: only Sunsama imports GitHub PRs as tasks ([Sunsama Help](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/integrations/github)), and nobody maps tracker hierarchies onto planner nesting in both directions. Fifth, **AI weighting from engineering signals** such as story points, severity, release dates or CI status has no owner. A general "AI prioritisation" feature does not count as a gap, because Morgen and Sunsama already ship reviewable AI day plans.

Two further openings are softer but useful. After Clockwise deleted its users' data, **guaranteed export and local-first storage** became a selling point you can state plainly. Pricing has room in the middle: to-do apps run $4–8/month and premium planners $15–49, so $8–12 with a usable free tier lands between them. Steelman the other side before you commit, though. Super Productivity is free and already covers Jira, GitHub and GitLab. Your edge over it has to be design, ADO, templates and nesting, not "dev integrations" in general. Incumbents can also close the ADO gap quickly once it pays. And a planner that connects to work trackers inherits workplace policy: Sunsama notes that some Atlassian organisations block user-installed apps ([Sunsama Help](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/integrations/jira)), and ADO org admins can disable third-party OAuth (the standard "log in with another service" authorisation) entirely ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/integrate/get-started/authentication/azure-devops-oauth)).

### Build GitHub first, Azure DevOps second, Jira third

All three APIs are free to use and feasible for one developer, but their difficulty differs. **GitHub is the easiest.** User tokens get 5,000 requests per hour, GitHub App installations scale to 12,500 ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/using-the-rest-api/rate-limits-for-the-rest-api)), webhooks are mature, and Sunsama's per-repo GitHub App is a production pattern you can copy. GitHub also lets you demonstrate the app publicly without needing an employer organisation's consent, which matters for a portfolio piece. The "PRs awaiting my review" queue is the differentiating feature to ship first.

**Azure DevOps is the hardest, and the most valuable.** ADO's own OAuth stopped accepting new apps in April 2025 and is scheduled for removal in 2026. New apps must use Microsoft Entra ID OAuth, which does not natively support personal Microsoft accounts (MSA) for the ADO resource ([Microsoft Learn, updated 3 Nov 2026](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/integrate/get-started/authentication/entra-oauth)). Plan for Entra OAuth plus a personal access token (PAT) fallback; the Raycast ADO extension already runs on PATs ([Raycast](https://www.raycast.com/willem_van_lent/ado-search)). Rate limits are generous per user, at 200 throughput units per 5 minutes ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/integrate/concepts/rate-limits)). Whether enterprise tenants will require admin consent is unverified.

**Jira comes last because competitors already cover it.** OAuth 2.0 (3LO) apps can be shared without a Marketplace listing, but users see an "unreviewed app" warning. Refresh tokens are single-use and expire after 90 days of inactivity ([Atlassian](https://developer.atlassian.com/cloud/jira/platform/oauth-2-3lo-apps/)). The main constraint is the points-based rate limit, enforced since 2 March 2026. Its Tier 1 quota is one global pool per app, shared by every customer ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/platform/evolving-api-rate-limits)). The often-quoted figure of 65K points per hour is unverified. At scale this forces caching and incremental `updated >=` queries. If your own day job runs on ADO, swap the first two steps so you use the app on real work from the start.

## Position the app as the calm personal layer over your team's tracker

Do not compete as another AI calendar; that field is crowded and SEO-driven. Do not compete on "calm and beautiful" alone either: Sunsama, Structured and Amie already occupy that ground. Position the app as **the personal planner that speaks your team's tracker**. Tickets, PRs and test passes come in automatically and nest as deep as the work goes. Reusable engineering bundles drop onto any day. When AI arrives, it suggests and never silently reshuffles, and it respects pinned tasks and busy events. That design answers the main complaint about Motion directly. The Manchester worker bee suits this framing if you read it as industriousness and craft rather than cuteness. It also supports gentle conventions: tasks "carried over" rather than overdue, and glanceable priorities. Ship an MCP server early, and lead with Linux and web support, which developers expect.

**One-line pitch:** *"The calm daily planner for developers and QA engineers: your tickets, PRs and test passes, nested as deep as the work goes, with release and regression checklists you can drop onto any day."*

## Conclusion

The research changes what your project needs to prove. The calendar-and-quick-add surface is already solved, so the portfolio story is engineering-grade sync. That means reliable, hierarchy-preserving two-way integration with GitHub and ADO, and handling the auth and rate-limit constraints most incumbents avoided. Those are exactly the skills a quality engineer can show credibly, and they double as the moat. The biggest open question is whether QA engineers will adopt test-pass and release bundles inside a personal planner; no community evidence confirms it. Before you build the template library in depth, run a cheap validation: a landing page plus posts in QA communities, or a dozen interviews with QE colleagues. Lock in Hexday's domains now, and commission a trademark search before any public launch.
