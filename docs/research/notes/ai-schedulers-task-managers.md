# AI Auto-Schedulers and Established Task Managers: Competitor Profiles (as of 15 Nov 2026)

Scope: Motion, Reclaim.ai, Clockwise, TickTick, Todoist, Amazing Marvin, Things 3, Microsoft To Do/Planner, plus adjacent planners and newcomers (Akiflow, Sunsama, Morgen, and 2025-26 entrants). Research date: 2026-11-15. Many third-party review sites in this space are run by competitors (Temporal, Carly, Lifestack, Saner.ai, Morgen, Ellie, Akiflow). They are flagged where used. Primary sources fetched: usemotion.com/pricing, reclaim.ai/integrations, Todoist 2026 changelog (Sep to Nov portion), todoist.com/pricing, ticktick.com/about/upgrade, amazingmarvin.com/pricing.

## Status: active, acquired, pivoted, shut down?

### Takeaway
Clockwise shut down on 27 March 2026 after a Salesforce acqui-hire. Reclaim is a Dropbox-owned product (acquired 2024) and is still active. Motion raised $75M in 2025 and repositioned toward an "agentic work suite for businesses", and its "AI Employees" tier looks like it has been wound back. Todoist, TickTick, Things 3 and Amazing Marvin are all active and shipping. Microsoft is consolidating work management into Planner (Project for the web retired in Aug 2025) while To Do stays, with less investment.

### Inferences
- Clockwise's exit (focus-time and calendar defence for engineering orgs, Atlassian a reported customer) has left a gap in the market. Reclaim has absorbed much of it. A developer planner could position itself as a "safe home" with data export, a direct lesson from Clockwise's data deletion.
- Motion is drifting upmarket toward SMB and business AI agents. That leaves individual developers, and small dev and QA teams wanting a simple personal planner, less well served by it.
- Microsoft's churn (Project for the web retired, a Planner redesign with complaints, To Do de-prioritised) suggests Microsoft-shop dev and QA users face instability. This could be an opening for an Azure DevOps-friendly planner.

- No primary Clockwise or Salesforce announcement was fetched. A TechRadar report is cited secondhand.

## How do tasks and calendar connect (calendar view, drag tasks onto calendar, auto-scheduling)?

### Takeaway
There are three tiers. Motion and Reclaim auto-place tasks into calendar time and reflow it. Todoist and TickTick have strong manual calendar views with drag-to-schedule, and Todoist significantly improved drag and duration handling in Sep to Oct 2026. Things 3, Microsoft To Do/Planner and Amazing Marvin have weaker or more manual calendar linkage. Marvin is the exception among these, offering a planning-centric "auto-scheduler" strategy.

### Cited Findings
**Motion**
- Auto-scheduling remains core ("smart scheduling" on its AI Calendar page, auto-scheduling on its AI Gantt page). — [usemotion.com/pricing](https://www.usemotion.com/pricing)
- A competitor says core task auto-scheduling and deadline rescheduling are "functionally unchanged" after the pivot. — [Temporal](https://temporal.day/blog/motion-ai-agents-individual-users-2026)
- Google Calendar and Outlook are supported. — [Temporal](https://temporal.day/blog/motion-ai-agents-individual-users-2026)
- User complaints:
  - A June 2026 App Store reviewer said tasks were scheduled on top of existing busy events. — [Carly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/motion-ai-review/)
  - Another said the plan "moved everything around constantly". — [Carly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/motion-ai-review/)
  - There is no chronotype or focus-pattern awareness, so tasks land in any open slot. — [Temporal](https://temporal.day/blog/motion-ai-agents-individual-users-2026)
  - Microsoft 365 requires admin consent, which blocked a user. — [Carly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/motion-ai-review/)
- Positive hands-on reviews describe auto-scheduling "that works". — [Dupple](https://dupple.com/reviews/motion); [Saner.ai](https://www.saner.ai/blogs/motion-reviews)

**Reclaim.ai**

**Adjacent planners**
- Akiflow: aggregates tasks for manual time-blocking. Its AI assistant "Aki" does not auto-schedule the day. — [Morgen blog](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/sunsama-vs-akiflow)

- Morgen: proposes time blocks for you to approve ("human-in-the-loop"). — [Morgen blog (self-interested)](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/sunsama-vs-akiflow)

### Inferences
- The new app's "drag tasks into and out of the calendar plus click-a-day quick-add" is now table stakes at Todoist and TickTick level. Todoist's late-2026 work (durations without dates, long-press-drag to create, calendar Plan sidebar with filters) is the closest benchmark to match or beat.
- The pain points with full auto-schedulers (Motion reshuffling constantly, overlapping busy events) argue for an opt-in, suggest-then-accept AI model like Morgen's, which fits a "gentle, elegant" design tone.
- TickTick flattening subtask hierarchy in calendar blocks is a specific UX weakness the new app could beat with deep nesting rendered in its calendar.

## AI features: prioritisation, task weighting, auto-scheduling, natural-language input — how do they work and what do users say?

### Takeaway
AI has converged on four things: natural-language and voice capture (Todoist Ramble, TickTick voice), chat assistants, agentic task creation (Microsoft Planner Agent, Motion AI), and calendar auto-placement (Motion, Reclaim). True AI prioritisation or "task weighting" is mostly done by deadline and priority heuristics inside the auto-schedulers. No product found offers learned task weighting aimed at developers. MCP support is spreading: TickTick, Reclaim's Jira 2.0 via Atlassian Rovo MCP, and Sunsama.

### Cited Findings
**Motion**
- Pro AI includes AI Chat, AI Projects & Tasks, AI Calendar & Meetings, AI Docs/Wiki/Notes, AI Task Planner, and AI Writer & Editor. — [usemotion.com/pricing](https://www.usemotion.com/pricing)
- Usage is metered by AI credits: 7,500 per seat per month on Pro AI and 15,000 on Business AI. Overage is 25¢ per 100 credits on Pro and 19¢ on Business. — [usemotion.com/pricing](https://www.usemotion.com/pricing)
- Users complain about price, billing and refunds (double $228 charges, trials converting), "all AI customer service", and a weak mobile app. — [Carly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/motion-ai-review/); [Saner.ai](https://blog.saner.ai/motion-reviews)

**Reclaim.ai**
- AI suite: AI Tasks, Focus Time, Habits, Planner and Assistant chat (see the calendar section). Tasks are placed according to priority, deadlines and scheduling preferences. — [reclaim.ai/integrations](https://reclaim.ai/integrations); [Reclaim review roundup](https://get-alfred.ai/blog/reclaim-pricing)

**Todoist**

- Todoist has long-standing natural-language date parsing (well established).
- There is no AI auto-scheduling or prioritisation.

### Inferences
- Nobody owns "AI task weighting for engineering work", for example weighting by story points, severity, release date or CI status. That is a defensible later-phase differentiator for the new app.

- MCP is becoming the integration surface for AI (TickTick MCP servers, Reclaim plus Atlassian Rovo MCP). Shipping an MCP server early would let developer users drive the planner from Claude, ChatGPT or IDE agents.

### Gaps

- Motion's internal prioritisation logic (priority levels, hard and soft deadlines) is not documented in the sources fetched.
- How TickTick's "AI assistant" actually works is unverified beyond the feature list.

## Integrations: Jira, Azure DevOps, GitHub, GitLab, Linear — native or Zapier, one-way or two-way?

### Takeaway
Only Reclaim has native Jira and Linear integrations among the schedulers. Its Jira integration is two-way at least for completion, plus a newer Rovo/MCP-based "2.0". None of the products has native Azure DevOps or GitLab integration. GitHub is native only in Todoist's directory ("GitHub Sync", one-way). Motion, Amazing Marvin and Linear-to-Todoist rely on Zapier, Make, Integrately or Pipedream. Things 3 and Microsoft To Do have essentially no dev-tool integrations. This is the clearest whitespace for the new app.

### Cited Findings
**Reclaim.ai**
- Native task integrations: Jira, Linear, Asana, Google Tasks, Todoist and ClickUp. No GitHub, GitLab or Azure DevOps. — [reclaim.ai/integrations](https://reclaim.ai/integrations)
- Jira sync is described as two-way: completing a task in Reclaim completes it in Jira. Jira and Linear are on paid tiers. — [Reclaim review roundups](https://theaiagentindex.com/agents/reclaim-ai); [usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/reclaim-ai-review/) (secondary)
- Jira setup requirements:
  - A Jira admin installs the app.
  - Issues need an Original Estimate, plus a due date, fix version or Sprint, before they sync. Missing fields are flagged.
  - Manual mode syncs issues only when you click "Schedule with Reclaim" in Jira. Automatic mode syncs them once the required fields exist.
  - With no Start Date, the Sprint start date is used.

  — [Reclaim help 6355276](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/6355276); [Reclaim help 6355524](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/6355524-jira-integration-settings); [Reclaim help 6355278](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/6355278)
- "Reclaim 2.0 Jira integration" requires the Starter plan or above, Atlassian Rovo, and an MCP callback allowlist. — [Reclaim help 15266233](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/15266233-reclaim-2-0-jira-integration)

## Task nesting depth (levels of subtasks)

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings

  - Remembered expansion state

**TickTick**
